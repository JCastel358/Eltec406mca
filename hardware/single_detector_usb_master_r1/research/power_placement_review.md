U10 sits at(34,-7). Its OUT13/14 pads are(35.45,-8.00/-8.50), and OUTS12 is directly below them. C11 is rotated90degrees: its positive pad is south, near OUTS; its ground pad is north. This is a useful orientation. C13 and C39 are additional local output ceramics, not replacements for the C11 sense anchor.

1. **Route OUTS first and preserve it.** Use a separate fine sense trace from U10.12 to C11.1. Route OUT13/14 power copper to that capacitor independently, then distribute the rail from the capacitor cluster. The sense branch must meet load-current copper at C11.1, not at U10.13. OUTS and OUT intentionally have the same net name, so ERC and ordinary autorouting are unable to enforce this behavior. A legal same-net short between adjacent pins12and13 can silently defeat Kelvin sensing. The protected 0.20mm locked seed and DSN obstacle have now passed a native-copy/real-router probe; [the routing note](kelvin_routing.md) documents the explicit export flag and independent native branch checks. This closes the seed mechanism, not the full-board power route or plane review.
2. **Keep the feedback ground local.** Return R39.2 and all three CSET ground pads to the C11/C13 local ground node with a quiet branch. Connect this node to the broad GND structure; a quiet branch is not a floating analog ground island. Keep input charging, USB, emitter-control and detector-master return currents out of that narrow branch. At100mA,20milliohms of common ground resistance creates2mV, already a material fraction of the4.64mV upper-voltage budget.
3. **Route the now-compact SET cluster before general routes.** The parent moved all three100nF capacitors and R10; the native distances in the table supersede the earlier relocation suggestion. Connect C15 with the shortest bypass loop, then the other two in parallel. Keep the R10/R39 junction private and short. Use a clean held-output guard around the high-impedance SET region where practical; do not run SCLK, optocoupler drive or master-gate traces above/below it.
4. **Use planes/broad copper for reservoir current.** C12 is almost24mm from C11+. A25mm-long,0.5mm-wide1oz copper trace is approximately25milliohms before vias and contacts, or3mV at120mA. This illustrative resistance supports using broad power copper rather than a long thin trace. Keep a continuous adjacent GND return and avoid a long common neck serving both reservoir current and SET ground.
5. **Protect the comparator loop.** U18's divider is low impedance, but its fast output must not run alongside its input/reference nets. Keep R31.2/R32.1 joined close to IN+3 and route reference from U17/C14 as a quiet branch to IN-4. C16 has now moved to give1.67mm direct VCC spacing with accepted courtyard placement. Give C16 ground and U18 ground nearby GND vias; its3.43mm ground-pad distance does not itself prove a short mounted return loop. Keep R16 and the RAW-node loads controlled rather than creating a large capacitive ground-referenced patch.
6. **Keep supplies distinct.** The detector/USB ground stays continuous. EM_GND must remain isolated across every copper layer, via field and mounting feature. USB_PRESENT_5V and ESP_3V3 must not receive copper from either battery regulator or the held reservoir.

These layout rules follow the [LT3041 pin and layout guidance](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/lt3041.pdf). Capacitor ESL depends on mounted loop geometry, not footprint approval alone.

## Exposed-pad and thermal checklist

- U10 exposed pad15 is centered(34,-7),1.7mm by3.3mm, onGND. Solder it and connect it directly to GND10/11; it is an electrical connection as well as a heat path.
- The actual footprint contains eight unnumbered paste windows of0.685mm by0.670mm. Their combined area is about65.4% of the exposed copper area. Preserve that windowing unless the assembler's stencil review specifies a change.
- Use broad copper from the exposed pad to continuous detector-GND planes. Add several short thermal connections/vias to inner and bottom GND. Ordinary unfilled via-in-pad can wick solder; use the assembler's qualified filled/capped process for in-pad vias, or place ordinary thermal vias just beyond the north/south exposed-pad ends and join with broad copper. A local four-via starting pattern aroundx33.6/34.4,y-9.3/-4.7 is geometrically plausible, subject to final clearance/drill rules and routing.
- Do not place a ground-plane split beneath U10, the C11 feedback loop, the comparator or their return paths. Keep the emitter-ground region out of this thermal structure.
- At7.3V input,5.1798V output and100mA output, pass loss is about0.212W. Adding the datasheet's13mA ground-current limit at that specified100mA test point gives roughly0.307W input-derived dissipation as a conservative illustration. Do not apply that13mA limit to arbitrary higher output current.
- Startup can momentarily require about0.5A while charging the reservoir, producing several watts of short-duration pass loss at the beginning of the ramp. Thermal shutdown is fault protection, not a normal startup target. Check repeated USB cycling, low-battery hardware retries and startup temperature rise with the actual board and battery lead impedance.

## Is4.64mV upper headroom useful?

It is enough to justify a measured laboratory prototype, but it is **not enough to claim guaranteed transient compliance or production interchangeability**. The nominal5.1798V setting has70.2mV headroom to the ADS1256 operating maximum. The4.64mV figure is the remaining margin at the stacked silicon/resistor/leakage acceptance corner. Actual prototypes may sit close to nominal, but that must be measured, not presumed.

The monitor detects undervoltage, not overvoltage. During a load release it can continue to report ready even if AVDD momentarily exceeds5.25V. Local MLCCs and C12 reduce that excursion but do not specify a maximum. The purchased module also contains10uH followed by nominal22uF at actual AVDD. For a simplified undamped LC and a10mA abrupt reduction in load, the incremental peak scale is deltaI*sqrt(L/C)=6.74mV. This is an illustrative mechanism, not a module prediction: DCR, capacitor bias, damping, regulator output impedance and load waveform are unverified. It shows why header-only steady-voltage readings cannot close the4.64mV corner.

For useful-conversion qualification, measure actual AVDD against the module's AGND during startup, conversion/PGA changes, PDWN, operation abort, USB loss/reapplication and battery retries. Require measured upper peak plus measurement uncertainty to remain below5.25V while conversions are accepted. Likewise maintain4.75V minimum while accepting conversions. An AC-coupled transient measurement must be combined with a sufficiently accurate DC measurement; ordinary oscilloscope DC accuracy can be larger than the entire remaining corner margin. Firmware fault handling does not detect high-rail excursions, so an unmeasured overshoot cannot simply be called an invalidated sample.

This is separate from overstress protection. ADS1256 specifies AVDD absolute maximum6V, versus its4.75..5.25V operating range; analog inputs have their own-0.3..AVDD+0.3V absolute range. Thus a brief5.26V supply by itself is an operating-specification failure, not proof that the6V AVDD absolute limit was crossed. It still gives no guarantee of conversion performance. Other carrier components and module parts must meet their own limits, and the AIN-versus-AVDD shutdown checks remain independent requirements. [ADS1256 electrical and absolute limits](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads1256.pdf).

No comparator substitution is selected here. Preserving the present reviewed architecture and measuring the actual room-temperature prototype is a useful next step. A prototype that cannot keep its real AVDD peak plus uncertainty below5.25V must not be presented as a qualified measurement instrument; changing only RSET downward would also require rechecking comparator startup/underdrive margins.
